    How to read a group

    A group is one start and one walk toward A. At the ★, the user says something new. The four cases are four different things the user could say there. Everything before the ★ is the same in all four.

    • A Original goal. The first instruction asks for it.
    • B New goal. Only the revision names it. The Continue case does not use B.
    Continue
    The user confirms A. The robot keeps going to A and stops there.
    Replace
    The user switches to B. The robot goes to B and stops there; reaching A no longer counts.
    Insert
    The user adds B before A. The robot goes to B first, then to A, and stops at A.
    Append
    The user adds B after A. The robot goes to A first, then to B, and stops at B. Stopping at A ends the case unsuccessfully.

    What changed since the H₃ pilot, following your decisions: the robot is inside a room when the floor under it belongs to that room in HM3D's labels, with no margin, and a room is a goal only when its floor and ceiling labels agree; reference paths aim at least 0.5 m into a room. When the nearest viewpoint gives a weak view of the goal, the reference path walks to a nearby viewpoint with a clearer one. B is always farther from the start than the distance walked before the ★, so a robot that moves freely cannot have reached A or B when a distance-triggered revision arrives. Astra now checks every goal view, at STOP in all four cases and at the visits of Insert and Append, for whether the goal is recognizable. As before: everything is on one floor; each goal accepts exactly LangMap's own list of objects, which can include related objects (a desk for “table”); the sentences are fixed templates; the robot has 500 actions per goal (after the ★, 500 for Continue and Replace and 1,000 for Insert and Append); the first half of the list gives A and B the same goal type, the second half two different types.

    Each case has its own map with its reference path: gray from the start to the ★, then blue while it heads to A and orange while it heads to B. Rooms are colored by the floor labels. Under the maps are the robot's camera views at the start, the ★, the visit (Insert and Append) and STOP, with how much of the view shows the goal (and, for a goal described by its room, the described feature, or “not measured” when no annotated object matches the description's words) and the evaluator's goal test there. The goal test itself uses only where the robot is, as in LangMap: a visit counts as soon as the robot is within 0.25 m of a LangMap view point of the object, or on the room's floor; the reference path then walks to that view point or room point and turns toward the goal, so its view comes a few actions later.

    Check the path, and check the goal views: the STOP view must show the final goal, and in Insert and Append the visit view must show the goal visited there. “In view” means recognizable: you could point to the goal and say what it is; a sliver at the edge of the image, or something seen only through a doorway that you can't identify, doesn't count. A described room needs one thing its description names; an object described by its room, the object and one named thing; an object with its own description, the object itself. Passing through or near a goal on the way to another is fine: only the visits a case requires, in their order, and the final STOP count.

    Mark a case Correct when the path starts at the start, reaches the ★ before A, goes to the right targets in the right order with each goal in view where it is visited and at STOP, and the sentences name those targets. Otherwise mark Problem and say why in the note. Mark Unsure when you can't tell.
